Mathematics is learned by doing. Lectures introduce new ideas, but real understanding only comes from working through problems yourself: applying definitions, testing strategies, and sometimes failing before you succeed. Because each topic builds on the last, small gaps in understanding can quickly grow into serious obstacles. Exercise classes, tutorials and office hours help, but they are not always enough, and not every student can attend them.

This is where good exercise and study books make a difference. They let students practice at their own pace, revisit difficult topics, and check their understanding whenever they need to. My books also include practice exams with model solutions, so students can prepare for their exams under realistic conditions.

Anyone who has studied mathematics or a related subject knows this moment:

> _I understand the problem, but I have no idea how to approach it._

Usually there are two options: give up, or look up the solution. Afterwards, many students realize they could have solved it themselves with just the right hint. That is why every exercise in my books comes not only with a detailed model solution, but also with a hint that points to the key idea without giving the answer away. Often, a small nudge is all it takes.
