teaching

Lectures and courses at the University of Tübingen and Martin Luther University Halle-Wittenberg.

Teaching has been part of my work since my studies. Today I regularly give lectures at the University of Tübingen and Martin Luther University Halle-Wittenberg, on topics ranging from mathematical problem solving to cryptography and software obfuscation.

Problem Solving Strategies

University of Tübingen · Winter semester 2026/27 · Lecture with exercise classes

An introduction to the art of mathematical problem solving. Working through problems of the kind posed in mathematical competitions, students build a broad repertoire of proof techniques, principles and heuristics and learn to apply them on their own. The course is designed as a workshop: part of the time is devoted to solving, discussing and presenting problems. The lecture notes were written together with Clemens Hlauschek.

Topics: Pólya’s problem-solving scheme, proof techniques (direct proof, contradiction, contraposition, induction), sets and mappings, elementary combinatorics and counting, the pigeonhole principle, invariance and monovariance, the extremal principle, colouring proofs, graph theory (including Sperner’s lemma, Brouwer’s fixed point theorem and matchings), games and winning strategies (Hex, Nim), advanced integration techniques, the subset-sum problem and the Merkle–Hellman cryptosystem, and foundations of probability.

Software Obfuscation and Deobfuscation with Mixed Boolean-Arithmetic Expressions

University of Tübingen · Summer semester 2026 · Lecture with exercise classes

Mixed Boolean-arithmetic (MBA) expressions combine Boolean and arithmetic operations and play a central role in protecting software against reverse engineering, as well as in breaking such protections. This course gives a rigorous, self-contained mathematical introduction to MBA expressions and their use in obfuscation and deobfuscation, with full proofs and carefully chosen examples that highlight both the strengths and the limitations of current techniques.

Topics: algebra and number theory (divisibility, Euclid’s algorithm, rings, quotient rings, computation in \(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\)), permutation polynomials, linear algebra over rings (modules, determinants, Hermite normal form), Boolean algebras and Boolean functions, linear and polynomial MBA expressions, MBA-based obfuscation (permutation polynomials, rewrite rules, complexity metrics, existing tools), and MBA-based deobfuscation (simplification rules, program synthesis, comparison of tools).

Mathematical Foundations of Cryptography

University of Tübingen · Winter semester 2025/26 · Lecture with exercise classes

An introduction to the mathematics behind modern cryptography, from historical ciphers to the public-key methods used today, together with the necessary algebra and computational number theory.

Topics: division with remainder, primes, greatest common divisors and the extended Euclidean algorithm, congruences and the Chinese remainder theorem, groups, cyclic groups and Euler’s totient function, classical ciphers and their cryptanalysis (Caesar, substitution and Vigenère ciphers, Kasiski and Friedman tests), the Diffie–Hellman key exchange and man-in-the-middle attacks, fast exponentiation, the discrete logarithm problem (baby-step giant-step, Pollard’s ρ method), RSA including attacks and parameter choices following the recommendations of the German Federal Office for Information Security (BSI), and digital signatures.

Mathematical Foundations of Cryptography

Martin Luther University Halle-Wittenberg · 2025 · Lecture with exercise classes

A course on the mathematical foundations of cryptography, covering number theory and algebra, classical encryption methods, the Diffie–Hellman key exchange, discrete logarithms and the RSA cryptosystem.

Nonlinear Analysis: Fixed Point and Variational Methods

Martin Luther University Halle-Wittenberg · Summer semester 2024 · Lecture with exercise classes

A course closely related to my doctoral research. It shows how many problems in analysis, such as nonlinear equations, initial and boundary value problems, equilibrium problems and obstacle problems, can be reformulated as fixed point problems or variational inequalities, and develops the tools to solve them.

Topics: the fixed point theorems of Banach, Brouwer and Schauder, the Leray–Schauder principle, compact operators, projections and best approximation in Hilbert spaces, variational inequalities in \(\mathbb{R}^d\) and in Hilbert spaces, the Lax–Milgram theorem, the theorem of Lions and Stampacchia, Minty’s lemma, and regularization methods.
